Cultural Competence Matrix

A Visualization Framework for Public Health Access in Marginalized Populations

R · ggplot2 · health-equity diagnostics · transferable methods

Overview

The Cultural Competence Matrix scores how accessible a health system is to a marginalized population across six evidence-based domains, then visualizes where access breaks down. The framework is population-agnostic; it is illustrated here with a documented field case — the Malayali of the Sitheri Hills, Tamil Nadu (author's MA Medical Anthropology dissertation) — alongside illustrative comparison populations.

Key findings

  • In the Malayali case, the state has built physical infrastructure (structural access and trust ≈ 2/3), but the system stays weak where it must adapt to the people — organizational representation, communication fit, and dignity ≈ 1/3 (hardware present, cultural software missing).
  • Across populations, capacity (what the system provides) often does not convert into realized, dignified use — urban unhoused adults show the widest conversion gap (+22), signaling access that exists on paper but fails in practice.
  • The six-domain matrix works as a transferable diagnostic: the same scoring pinpoints which level (organizational, structural, or clinical) each population's access breaks down at, generalizing well beyond the case.

Theoretical grounding

Domains draw on peer-reviewed literature: Betancourt et al.'s tri-level model of cultural competence (organizational / structural / clinical), the Cochrane review of cultural-competence education (Horvat et al.), language-concordance evidence (Diamond et al.), structural competency (Malone et al.), medical mistrust (Dong et al.), and pluralistic care-seeking (Chowdhury et al.), with Andersen's Behavioral Model framing the capacity-vs-realized split.
